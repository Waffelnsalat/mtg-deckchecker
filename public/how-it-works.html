Method

How the deck analysis works

The analyzer combines card text, type lines, Commander context, known package patterns, and external card data to produce a structured deck read. The output should be treated as a useful review, not an absolute verdict.

1. Card resolution

Decklists are parsed into sections such as commander, mainboard, companion, attraction deck, and sticker sheets. Card names are resolved through Scryfall data so the app can work with oracle text, mana value, color identity, type line, and images.

2. Role tagging

Cards are tagged for practical deck roles such as ramp, draw, tutors, protection, removal, recursion, finishers, combo support, and drawbacks. Similar tags are grouped for display so a card does not become unreadable.

3. Strategy detection

The tool compares the command zone, mainboard cards, and detected role packages to infer the likely strategy. If several strategies are plausible, the app asks which one you intend to play and recalculates against that plan.

4. Power and bracket read

Power is split into speed, consistency, interaction, resilience, closing power, and mana quality. Bracket read then combines that profile with Commander barometers such as Game Changers, compact combos, extra turns, and mass land denial. Bracket 1 prioritizes theme, Brackets 2 and 3 differ in card quality and focus, Brackets 3 and 4 differ in speed, and Bracket 5 requires a competitive metagame mindset. The expected minimum number of turns before anyone wins or loses is generally 9, 8, 6, and 4 for Brackets 1 through 4; cEDH games can end on any turn. The tool estimates a fit from a decklist and cannot verify actual win turns or player intent.

5. Weak matchups

Weaknesses compare what the deck depends on against what it can answer. Graveyard decks are checked against graveyard hate, combat decks against pillowfort and wipes, spell-chain decks against Rule of Law effects, and high-salt decks against table focus.

6. Table salt

Salt risk is separate from power. It estimates whether a deck may draw social pressure because of commonly disliked cards or play patterns such as hard locks, mass land denial, extra turns, oppressive tax engines, or compact win lines.

MTG Deckchecker Analysis logic is heuristic and should be reviewed by the player.
